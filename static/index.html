When a payment fails, a founder should know what to do in under a minute.

Money moves, cards get declined, and payments either settle or fail. Settle focuses on the moment a payment fails, when a bank transfer comes back or a card is declined, and turns it into a plain-English explanation plus a one-click fix. Finance teams also see which payments are at risk of failing before they go out.

The answer first

Situation
Startups pay vendors, move money between accounts, and spend on company cards from one banking platform. Most bank-to-bank payments in the US travel over ACH, the network banks use to send money between accounts. Most of them go through without anyone noticing.
Complication
When an ACH payment fails, the bank sends back a return code. For example, R03 means the receiving bank couldn’t find the account. Card failures come back as a short decline message. The founder doesn’t know what the code means, who has to fix it, or whether trying again makes it worse. So they open a support ticket, and a vendor or employee waits.
Question
How do we make a failed payment something a customer can fix on their own, fast, without losing trust in where their money is?
Answer
Explain every failure in plain English, name who acts, give the next step in one click, and catch the predictable failures before money leaves the account.

Who it’s for

The founder who pays the bills. Early-stage, no finance team, pays vendors and contractors from the same account. Wants to know if money is safe and what to do next.

The finance lead or fractional controller. Runs payouts in batches and closes the books. Cares about which payments will settle on time and which need a retry before month-end.

Success, defined before building

Median time from failure to fixPrimaryFrom the moment a return or decline posts to the moment the customer resends, edits details, or contacts the vendor.
Repeat-failure rate on retried paymentsSecondaryRetries that fail again for the same reason. If this goes up, the guidance is wrong.
Failed-payment support tickets per 1,000 paymentsSecondaryThe cost signal for Ops. Should fall without customers going quiet on real problems.
Payouts sent after a bank-details warningGuardrailPre-flight warnings must not be ignored by habit. Track the false-positive rate so warnings stay rare and believable.

What v1 does, and what it doesn’t

In

  • Plain-English explanations for the most common ways a payment fails: the vendor’s account is closed, can’t be found, is frozen, or can’t take transfers; your other bank is low on funds or blocked the transfer; and common card declines.
  • A clear owner on every failure: you, your vendor, your other bank, or support.
  • One next step per failure, including a drafted message to the vendor or bank.
  • Pre-flight checks on scheduled payouts: changed bank details, first payment to a new vendor, and a balance that won’t cover the batch.

Out

  • Automatic retries. A person approves every resend.
  • Consumer accounts and consumer return rules.
  • Any change to how payments are routed or settled.

Bringing Compliance and Ops in early

  • Compliance reviews the explanation copy before launch, since it describes why money moved and must not imply fault or give legal advice. Also signs off on retry limits.
  • Risk and fraud own the rules for the bank-details warning, so the pre-flight check matches what they already watch for.
  • Support and Ops get the same explanations in their tools, so a customer who still writes in hears the same answer.

Where AI fits, and where it doesn’t

The reason for a failure comes from a fixed library mapped to each return code and decline reason. It never comes from a model, because a wrong explanation about money is worse than a slow one.

AI does the writing around that fact: drafting the message to a vendor or bank in the customer’s voice, and in a full version, grouping support tickets about failed payments to find the next codes worth covering. AI never moves money or triggers a retry.

How I’d validate it

  • Five short conversations with founders and finance leads about the last payment that failed on them, and what they did next.
  • Tag a month of failed-payment support tickets by code, to confirm which six or seven reasons cover most of the volume.
  • Launch to a small group, compare time-to-fix and ticket rate against customers without Settle, then decide whether to expand the code library or the pre-flight checks.

Open questions

  • Should the vendor get a link to update their own bank details, and what verification would that need?
  • Which pre-flight warning has the best signal-to-noise for a two-person startup?
  • Does a finance lead want this per payment, or as one daily digest?
